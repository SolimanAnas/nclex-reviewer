PART 1 — How to Prioritize

Golden Rule

“Severe C comes before stable B” — an unstable problem beats a stable one, even lower in the ABC order.

7 Key Principles

  1. ABCs — Airway (choking, obstruction) → Breathing (distress, hypoxia) → Circulation (bleeding, shock, arrest). Life threats first.
  2. Maslow — physiological needs (O2, fluids, nutrition) before safety & emotional. Dyspnea beats anxiety about a diagnosis.
  3. Acute > Chronic — sudden chest pain / bleeding beats long-term diabetes management.
  4. Unstable > Stable — hypotension, altered mental status get seen now; stable clients can wait.
  5. Risk of harm — greatest risk first: anaphylaxis, PE before mild pain.
  6. Least restrictive first — deep breathing before an anxiolytic, unless immediate action is needed.
  7. Time-sensitive — stroke, MI: treatment window drives priority.

5 Quick Frameworks

  • ABCs blocked airway before fractured arm
  • Safety first fall / seizure risk before routine care
  • Acute over chronic acute chest pain before stable HTN
  • Systemic over local sepsis/shock before wound infection
  • Actual over potential active bleeding before risk of infection

Pitfalls

  • Overlooking ABCs — routine meds never come before a life threat.
  • Fixating on chronic conditions — stable diabetes/HTN rarely wins over a new acute change.

PART 2 — My Priority Notes (by System)

Neuro

  • PRIORITY Meningitis with signs of meningeal irritation → start treatment (antibiotics) now.

  • PRIORITY Chronic HTN + epistaxis + blurred vision → hypertensive encephalopathy.

  • PRIORITY Back pain + new urinary retention / incontinence + saddle anesthesia → cauda equina syndrome — emergency.

  • PRIORITY Dementia + new-onset restlessness → delirium (acute change).

  • PRIORITY ALS: respiratory muscle weakness, dysphagia, difficulty speaking, muscle weakness with intact mind → respiratory failure is the major life threat; dysphagia = aspiration risk.

  • EXPECTED Absence seizure, no physical injury → not a priority.

Cardiovascular

  • PRIORITY Warmth & pain in leg post-op → DVT.

  • PRIORITY PAD + acute abdominal pain radiating to the (lower) back → AAA / aortic dissection.

  • PRIORITY New S3 sound → fluid overload / heart failure, risk of cardiogenic shock. In older adults it requires prompt evaluation.

  • PRIORITY MAOI + pseudoephedrine → headache → hypertensive crisis. Detail: MAOIs (isocarboxazid [Marplan], phenelzine [Nardil], tranylcypromine [Parnate]) + drugs that raise BP (decongestants: pseudoephedrine, oxymetazoline) → hypertensive crisis → hemorrhagic stroke, death. Headache is a common early symptom.

  • PRIORITY MAP below 60 (normal 70–100) → critical, act now.

  • REF BNP normal < 100 pg/mL → if high, the dyspnea is from heart failure.

Respiratory

  • PRIORITY Obstructive sleep apnea → airway risk.

  • REF Asthma peak-flow zones: Green 80–100% · Yellow 50–79% · Red <50%.

Post-op & Surgical

  • PRIORITY Third spacing after abdominal surgery (low urine output) → fluid shifts intravascular → interstitial/third space → ↓ circulating volume → ↓ renal perfusion → oliguria.

  • PRIORITY Drowsiness after surgery → risk of aspiration.

  • PRIORITY Nausea & dry heaving after surgery → high risk for dehiscence.

  • PRIORITY Post-op / fracture with sudden confusion + desaturation → fat embolism.

  • PRIORITY Large body cast → bowel obstruction (cast / SMA syndrome), reduced peristalsis.

  • PRIORITY External fixator + fever → osteomyelitis (serious bone infection).

  • PRIORITY Post-thyroidectomy + positive Trousseau's sign → hypocalcemia.

  • PRIORITY Tonsillectomy → hemorrhage risk for 14 days; avoid vigorous sports.

  • EXPECTED Right shoulder pain after laparoscopy → phrenic nerve irritation from CO2 → Sims position, deep breathing.

  • EXPECTED Right shoulder pain in cholecystitis → expected.

  • EXPECTED Paralytic ileus 24–72 h post-op → expected; give fiber, fluids, mobilization.

Infection & Sepsis

  • PRIORITY Fever of unknown origin + low PaCO2 → SIRS → sepsis.

  • PRIORITY Sepsis suspected (e.g. high fever) → start antibiotics within 1 hour.

  • PRIORITY Sepsis + petechiae → DIC.

  • PRIORITY Older adults — immunosenescence: atypical infection signs (hypothermia, altered mental status, leukopenia) → report immediately.

  • PRIORITY Acute pyelonephritis → aggressive IV fluids + IV antibiotics; a patent IV line is the priority.

  • PRIORITY Septic arthritis of hip: fever, pain, swelling, refuses to bear weight, hip externally rotated (to relieve pressure) → immediate treatment — permanent joint damage.

  • PRIORITY Mononucleosis + LUQ pain → splenic rupture until proven otherwise; pain radiating to left shoulder (Kehr sign).

Endocrine

  • REF SIADH vs DI → SIADH = too much ADH → water retention, hyponatremia, concentrated urine. DI = too little ADH → massive dilute urine, hypernatremia / dehydration.

OB & Pediatrics

  • PRIORITY Pregnant + RUQ abdominal pain → HELLP syndrome (liver swelling, elevated liver enzymes).

  • PRIORITY Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (3rd trimester): generalized pruritus, worse on hands & feet, NO rash → increased fetal complications.

  • PRIORITY Neonate: hypothermia + poor feeding + lethargy + respiratory distress → neonatal sepsis; respiratory distress / apnea is the priority. Draw cultures (C/S), then start antibiotics.

  • PRIORITY Infant: fever, high-pitched cry, sunset eyes, bulging fontanelles → meningitis.

  • PRIORITY Testicular torsion → ischemia & necrosis.

  • EXPECTED Fifth disease (parvovirus B19) “slapped cheek” red facial rash → harmless unless other conditions.

Meds, Blood & Other

  • PRIORITY Red pinpoint rash after sulfa → thrombocytopenia (blood dyscrasia) → petechiae that do not blanch.

  • PRIORITY Rhabdomyolysis (extreme exercise, drugs / toxins e.g. cocaine): dark urine, muscle pain, markedly elevated CK, high K+ → risk of acute kidney injury (CK > 15,000).

  • PRIORITY High temperature after exertion + lethargy → heat stroke.

  • REF Morphine: assess pain 30 minutes after injection.

  • REF INR: normal 0.8–1.2 · therapeutic 2–3 · up to 3.5 with mechanical valve · >4 → hold next dose.
